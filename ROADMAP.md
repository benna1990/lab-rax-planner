# Roadmap

Status on 15 October 2026 and what comes next. Written so a new session (for example in Claude Code) can pick it up.

## Done

- Planner UI: parts library (devices, panels, top mounts, frame & extras, custom), front view at real scale, light/dark theme, share link.
- The back of the rack: a Back view to place panels on the rear posts (vents, fan panels, keystones, power), shown in 3D, in the cooling estimate (rear vents and fans at their own height), the lists and the PDF. A front and a rear part at the same height that together are deeper than the rack are marked.
- Catalog of ~500 community parts with links, authors and heights (≈ when estimated). About 150 were added on 15 October 2026 from a MakerWorld search sweep, and 64 more from a second sweep the same day (power, patch panels, thin clients, firewall boxes).
- Whole-U panels snap to the EIA hole pattern; ½U panels fit either half.
- 3D view (three.js r128) with side panels toggle, selection, and loading your own STL/3MF onto a part. The frame is modelled after the photos of the real Lab Rax (bevelled rings, arched handles, brass inserts).
- Custom part generator with STL export: blank, vent (slots/hex), keystone (1–12 ports, 1–2 rows), label plate, shelf sized to a device, fan panel (1–5× 40 mm in 1U, 1–2× 80 mm in 2U or 1× 120 mm in 3U, honeycomb grille or open ring; counts as a fan in the cooling estimate and the shopping list), Neutrik D-type panel (1–7 cut-outs per row, 1U or 2U) and cable pass-through (slot or brush slot, 30–200 mm, ½U or 1U).
- Build pack ZIP: custom STLs, PDF build plan, print list and shopping list (CSV), README and the layout as a rack file.
- Rack file: **Save as file** writes the layout as `<name>-<h>u.rack.json` (the share link's compact format with a format name and version); **Open file** reads it back with Undo. Share links (`#c=`, older `#l=`) still open, and both now keep fan directions and the room temperature.
- Print bed check: pick a printer (presets for Bambu, Prusa and Creality, or your own size); the print list, the wizard, the PDF checklist and the CSV mark prints with parts too big for the bed or that only fit diagonally (`printers.js`, `tools/test-printers.mjs`). Frame and catalog sizes are estimates; see README, "Print bed check".
- Shopping list with Amazon search links and an affiliate tag setting.
- Airflow estimate: heat per part (rule of thumb, catalog `watts` or the user's value), stack and fan flow, air temperature rise, recommendations with their expected effect, and an Airflow view. Hover tooltips replace text on the panels; the build pack has a download list.
- Every part looks like its model in the library, the front view and the 3D view (`shapes.js`): a `shape` per catalog entry (Pi blades, keystones, drive sleds, fans, vents, sockets, screens, device windows), real sizes and front I/O for all 142 devices from the makers' spec sheets (28 estimated), access points and fan or mesh tops, and extras that show on the frame (patterned or fan side panels, handles, feet, casters, drawer, lit logo, corner keystones, deeper sides). Hovering a library row shows a larger drawing with the device's size. `tools/looks.html` shows every part.
- Power budget (`power.js`, Power & network section, PDF block, Copy list): total draw from the same watts as the cooling estimate, energy per year, PoE budget and ports per switch with injector or bigger-switch advice, network ports against switch and router ports, UPS runtime for a size in VA or Wh. Catalog fields `poe`, `poeIn`, `poeOptional`, `ports`, `eth` on the PoE switches, gateways, routers, U7 Pro, Cloud Key and Raspberry Pis.
- Public repo at https://github.com/benna1990/lab-rax-planner, live on GitHub Pages at https://benna1990.github.io/lab-rax-planner/, with the catalog validator as a GitHub Action.

## Next

1. **Measure the original frame.** Download the Lab Rax 3MF from MakerWorld into `reference/` (git ignores it) and measure ring width and height, bevels, post section, handle and feet. Update `FRAME` in `view3d.js` and the table in `README.md`.
2. **Check estimated heights (≈).** 218 of 430 mounts and panels have `"estimated": true`. Open each model page and set the real height; most of the sweep entries came from search results only. From the second sweep, check these first: 2261662 (Wyse 5070; the page also mentions a 19″ rack), 442928 (½U keystone panel with a 30 mm plate, may need 1U), and 1427297, 1845503 and 1994910 (numbers seen only on the 3dgo.app mirror).
3. **Test-print the custom parts.** One ½U keystone panel and one 1U vent panel; adjust `KS_W`, `KS_H`, `HOLE_D` and `PANEL_W` in `parts.js` if needed. Then a 1U fan panel with 40 mm fans, a D-type panel with a real Neutrik connector and a ½U brush slot (do the 0.8 mm fingers bend enough in PETG?); the numbers are `FANS`, the `D_` constants and `passH` in `parts.js`.
4. **More custom parts.** Pi tray. A D-type panel needs at least 1U: the Neutrik flange is 31 mm tall and a ½U plate 21.4 mm.
5. **Check the looks against the models.** sbc7, the mklements panels, the UCG-Max, USW-Lite-8-PoE, Lenovo Tiny, Mac mini and the U7 Pro mounts were checked against their photos; the other shapes follow from the labels. Open `tools/looks.html`, compare with each model page and tune its `shape` (keystones left or right, window style, number of units). Better: download the model into `reference/` as `<id>.3mf` or `.stl` and run `node tools/measure.mjs <id>`; it reports the height, screw holes, openings, depth and tilt and how much of the drawn openings match. Measured so far (15 October 2026): `vent1`, `keystone`, `mw1385530`, `sbc7` (holder and the Pi 4 and Pi 5 blades) and `u7pro45`; their shapes now follow the models. The Lab Rax panels are now drawn at their measured size (catalog `fit: "labrax"`): 2.45 mm short of their U's (42 mm for 1U, 86.45 for 2U) with three 10 × 7 mm slots per U, and the ½U ones 22.6 mm, turned over in the upper half of a U. Still open: two ½U Lab Rax panels in one U overlap by 3.6 mm in the drawing (2 × 22.6 is more than a U; check on a print whether they really can't share one), the panels are 252 mm wide where the planner draws 250, and the real top panel is 228 × 171.5 where the 3D frame draws 222 × 182 (see step 1). Ten devices are wider than the 222 mm opening (Cisco 2960-C 269 mm, FRITZ!Box 7590 250, Zyxel GS1900-8 250, MG-108 240, EdgeRouter 4 229, RB4011 228, ER707-M2 226, FRITZ!Box 7362 SL and 7560 226); their mounts hold them some other way, so check how. Device sizes now set the depth in 3D; mount `depth` is only needed when a shelf differs.
6. **Rear view and cabling** in 3D.
7. **Catalog gaps.** The first October sweep found RB5009, Cloud Key Gen2+, UNVR Instant and HDHomeRun (with a Pi 5). The second sweep added 64 parts: PDUs and power strips, keystone, fibre and Neutrik D-type patch panels, shelf drawers, IKEA Dirigera, Starlink Gen 2, thin clients (Wyse 3040/5070, HP t620/t630/t640, Futro S740), firewall boxes (Topton/CWWK, Qotom, Protectli VP2420, Firewalla Gold) and obibi's bolted +50 mm sides (2350077). Still none for UDR/UDR7, mini UPS units (Printables 1208746 fits an APC Back-UPS 650, height unknown), TP-Link Deco, Starlink Gen 3/Mini, GL.iNet Flint 2, Netgate, Futro S920/S940 or HP t730/t740; the USW Pro Max is 281 mm wide and doesn't fit. The "Lab Rax U7 Pro mount remix" lead is Loonie Merker's 3217818, already listed. Open leads seen by title but without an id: matataa's "Lab Rax PWN controller top panel with 120mm fan", zaqu's "Neutrik D Series and Keystone 10-inch Panel" (a remix of 825894), Janjd's "Labrax 10" Rack mount for HP ThinClient (t620Plus)", and 1U drawers by Patrick and by usefulhobbies. No D-type panel by mklements turned up.
8. **Real heat figures.** Add `watts` to common devices in `catalog.json` (gateways, PoE switches, mini PCs, NAS) so the airflow estimate uses measured values instead of rules of thumb, and check the estimate against a thermometer in a real rack.
9. **Power budget data.** `poe`, `poeIn` and `ports` are filled for the common switches, gateways, routers, the U7 Pro, Cloud Key and Raspberry Pis. Still open: the "UniFi PoE injector" (mw1214393) is a 46 × 33 × 86 mm unit that matches neither the 30 W PoE+ adapter (93 × 62 × 35 mm) nor the PoE++ one, so it has no `poe` yet; the entry called "TP-Link G2206P" is an IP-COM G2206P-4-63W (4 PoE ports, 58 W), so its name may need fixing; "Netgear GS305 / GS305P", "Omada ER707-M2 / ES210XPP-M2" and "Cisco Catalyst 2960-C / 3560-CX" cover PoE and non-PoE models, so they have no `poe`. Budgets that depend on the adapter (USW-Ultra 42/52/202 W, USW-Flex-2.5G-8-PoE 46–196 W, USW-Flex 20/46 W) use the documented default; builders can enter their own. The cooling estimate could add the PoE power supply's loss (about 10 % of the PoE load, which the Power section now knows) to the switch's heat.
